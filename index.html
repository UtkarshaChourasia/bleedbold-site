Why this exists

A gap nobody was filling

BleedBOLD started with a simple observation from home: in many families, periods are still handled in whispers, girls miss school during their cycle, and menstrual cups, the one product that could actually solve this, aren't sold anywhere nearby.

We run in person sessions in schools and community halls that cover the biology, the hygiene, and the plain fact that this is normal, not shameful. We follow every session with menstrual cup distribution so the conversation turns into something a girl can actually use that week, and use again for the next fifteen years.

Nobody should have to choose between school and their period.

Why a cup, not a pad

The thing girls actually need, but can't buy

Not on any shelf

Walk into almost any pharmacy, mall kiosk, or corner shop in a small town and you'll find pads. Menstrual cups are a different story, most places simply don't stock them. For many girls we work with, we're the first place they've ever been offered one.

Lasts 15 plus years

One cup, cared for properly, holds up for over fifteen years. That's not close to a monthly pad purchase, it's a decision a girl makes once and is done with for most of her reproductive life.

Checked by a gynecologist

Every cup we distribute is reviewed by a gynecologist before it reaches anyone. Quality control on menstrual products varies a lot depending on where they're made, so we verify it ourselves rather than take a supplier's word for it.

First time with a cup

What it is, and how it's used

Most girls we meet have never seen a menstrual cup before, so every session starts here.

  • ✓ Made of soft medical grade silicone, not plastic or latex
  • ✓ Sits low in the body and collects flow instead of absorbing it
  • ✓ Can be worn for up to 12 hours before it needs emptying
  • ✓ Rinsed with water between uses, sterilized in boiling water between cycles
  • ✓ One cup replaces roughly 2,400 disposable pads over its lifetime

How it runs

Three steps, same order every time

Get into the room

We partner directly with a school or a community group willing to give us an hour with their girls or women, no middlemen, no paperwork chain.

Say it plainly

Biology, hygiene, what's normal and what isn't, delivered without euphemism, in whatever language the room actually speaks.

Leave real supply

Every session ends with menstrual cups going home with the people in that room, not a pamphlet, not a promise for later.

On the ground

What a session actually looks like

Two moments from recent outreach, one mid conversation, one right after supplies went out.

School session

Straight talk, no euphemisms

We sit with students in their own school courtyard and walk through what's actually happening in their bodies, what's normal, and what to do about pain, irregularity, and hygiene, in language they'd use with each other.

Community distribution

Education backed by supplies

Talking about periods openly matters, but it doesn't fix an empty shelf at home. Every session we run is paired with a menstrual cup distribution round so families leave with something reusable that lasts years, not a monthly cost.
