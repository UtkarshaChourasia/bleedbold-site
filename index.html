Why this exists

A gap nobody was filling

BleedBOLD started with a simple observation from home: in many families, periods are still handled in whispers, girls miss school during their cycle, and menstrual cups, the one product that could actually solve this, aren't sold anywhere nearby.

We run in person sessions in schools and community halls that cover the biology, the hygiene, and the plain fact that this is normal, not shameful. We follow every session with menstrual cup distribution so the conversation turns into something a girl can actually use that week, and use again for the next fifteen years.

Nobody should have to choose between school and their period.

Why a cup, not a pad

The thing girls actually need, but can't buy

Not on any shelf

Walk into almost any pharmacy or corner shop in a small town and you'll find pads. Menstrual cups are a different story, most places simply don't stock them. For many girls we work with, we're the first place they've ever been offered one.

Lasts 15 plus years

One cup, cared for properly, holds up for over fifteen years. That's not close to a monthly pad purchase, it's a decision a girl makes once and is done with for most of her reproductive life.

Checked by a gynecologist

Every cup we distribute is reviewed by a gynecologist before it reaches anyone. Quality control on menstrual products varies a lot, so we verify it ourselves rather than take a supplier's word for it.

Part three, putting it in

Five steps, in order

01
Wash your hands, and the cup
Plain soap and clean water is enough. Wetting the rim helps it slide in.
02
Get comfortable first
Sit on the edge of a seat, squat, or stand with one foot raised. Tense muscles make this harder, so take a breath and go slowly.
03
Hold the fold tightly
Keep it folded until it's fully in. If it springs open in your fingers, that's fine, just fold it again.
04
Aim back, not up
Guide it low and angled towards the base of your spine. It sits lower than a tampon, so it does not need to go far.
05
Let it open, then check the seal
Run a finger around the rim to feel for dents, or turn the cup a half circle. A good seal is what keeps it from leaking.

The worries we hear most

Asked in every single session

Can it get lost inside me?

No. It stays in the vaginal canal, which is a closed space only a few inches long. There is nowhere else for it to go.

Will it hurt?

It shouldn't. Discomfort almost always means the fold slipped, the angle was too upright, or the muscles were tense. Stop, breathe, and try again.

Can I use it as a virgin?

Yes. A cup is worn internally, and using one is a personal choice, not a statement about anything else. Our sessions cover this openly.

What if there's no privacy at home?

A cup helps here, it only needs emptying twice a day, so you can manage it morning and night rather than during school hours.

Can I sleep and play with it in?

Yes, all night and through sport. Nothing shifts as long as the seal is good.

What if I get it wrong?

Nothing breaks. Most people need two or three cycles before it feels routine, and that is completely normal.
